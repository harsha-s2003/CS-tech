CASE STUDY

IoT-Sensor-Based Monitoring System for Rural Water Supply – SWSM Maharashtra

Water & Wastewater Emerging Tech

The Assignment

State Water & Sanitation Mission (SWSM), Maharashtra required a digital monitoring framework to improve visibility of rural water distribution across villages, districts and the state level.

The objective was to monitor rural water supply in near real time, enabling better tracking of service-delivery uptime, minimisation of water losses and more equitable distribution of water across rural water supply schemes.

Our Approach

CS TECH Ai implemented an integrated monitoring solution combining IoT sensors, GIS-based asset mapping and centralized command and control capabilities to provide greater visibility of water supply infrastructure and its operation.

The engagement included:

  • GIS mapping of rural water supply assets
  • Integration of IoT sensors with water supply infrastructure
  • Centralized Control & Command Centre setup
  • Near real-time monitoring of equipment operation
  • Asset benchmarking across multiple locations
  • GIS-based asset and operational visualization
  • Monitoring of water distribution and service delivery
  • Centralized visibility across village, district and state levels

By integrating field-level IoT data with spatial asset information, the solution enabled water supply infrastructure and equipment performance to be viewed and monitored through a common digital environment.

Impact

Enabled end-to-end visibility of rural water supply across village, district and state levels.
Supported near real-time monitoring and event response across water supply infrastructure.
Improved operational visibility and decision-making efficiency.
Supported more equitable water distribution and water-loss minimisation.
Enabled data-driven and evidence-based planning for new schemes and modifications to existing schemes.
Supported improved life of rural water supply schemes through better asset visibility and monitoring.
Strengthened transparency and accountability in rural water supply management.
